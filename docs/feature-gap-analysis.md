# Chat feature gaps and landing-page review

Reviewed October 7, 2026 against this repository and current Google documentation. This is a source-grounded capability comparison, not a claim of pixel identity or real-device acceptance. Google's availability varies with account, plan and rollout. Our product remains an independent messenger for groups of at most 30 people; Google sign-in does not import Google Chat conversations.

## Landing-page judgment before this change

The existing page is readable and calm: a strong heading, one clear sign-in action, a recognizable conversation illustration, feature sections and concise FAQs. Local fonts, rounded surfaces and a restrained palette fit the intended visual direction. Its weakness is that the most prominent product illustration looks interactive while its composer and reaction do nothing. Generic benefit copy also makes visitors work to discover the concrete use case.

The improvement is a clearly labelled, local-only chat preview. Visitors should be able to send a short sample message, toggle a reaction and reset. Motion should acknowledge those actions, preserve transcript/composer geometry, and stop under reduced-motion preferences. No unattended conversation animation, fake typing urgency or invented delivery promise is needed. The real sign-in action must remain separate from the preview. A preview is product explanation, not evidence that cross-device delivery works.

Review dimensions: hierarchy and CTA visibility; readable text and contrast in both themes; no horizontal overflow at phone widths; keyboard order and accessible names; bounded text and message count; stable layout; inert state restored by Reset; no private persistence or chat writes; reduced motion; and useful FAQ answers about identity, invitations, draft recovery and file limits. Automated assertions and reviewed screenshots support these decisions; they cannot establish that every visitor finds the page appealing. A short usability exercise with first-time visitors is the appropriate next subjective check.

Google's product page uses a concise hero, product UI imagery and sections explaining capabilities. Its AI, large-space, integration and enterprise claims are outside our implemented product and should not appear in our landing copy. [Google Chat product page](https://workspace.google.com/products/chat/).

## Landing implementation and verification

The implemented change replaces the inert illustration with `LandingChatPreview`: a 160-character composer, an explicitly illustrative reply, a reversible raised-hands reaction and Reset. The transcript is capped at eight messages. Short message-entry and control transitions respond to interaction; reduced-motion preferences disable those transitions. The preview neither posts messages nor persists its contents. Supporting copy explains the 30-person limit and that invitations require the inviter to share the app link.

The dedicated acceptance run passed 20 Chromium/WebKit cases without updating baselines, comparing 24 reviewed screenshots at 320, 390 and 1,440 pixel widths across both themes and initial/sent states. Assertions also cover contrast, control size, overflow, focus, Enter/Shift+Enter, composition input, literal rendering of markup, bounded history, reset/reload, reduced motion and absence of private writes. Six existing landing checks and six reducer unit cases also passed. These are local browser-engine results; they do not claim physical-device acceptance.

Visual inspection found and corrected a narrow-screen initial scroll position that clipped the top of the transcript. The final initial/reset state begins at the top; after sending, the scrollable history exposes the latest reply. The result is more demonstrative and readable than the previous static illustration, while the sign-in action stays distinct. The screenshot baselines establish regressions against this reviewed implementation, not pixel equivalence with Google's marketing page.

## Priority for a 30-person product

Additional visual follow-up recorded October 7: compare the conversation header's Chat/Shared tabs against Google Chat in idle, hover, keyboard-focus and selected states, in both themes. The supplied 3:21 PM screenshot shows a prominent rectangular Shared fill beside the underlined Chat tab. Review fill contrast, corner geometry, header margins, tab gaps, text size and line height, label/icon spacing, underline position and state transitions using paired screenshots at matching viewport and zoom before changing the baseline. This item is pending the current coverage work; it is not verified Google parity.

The follow-up measured two sidebar inconsistencies in the current app: all-collapsed fine-pointer headings had 36px and 44px top intervals, and coarse-pointer DM/Spaces headings began 4px left of Shortcuts. The repair uses equal collapsed intervals within each pointer mode and aligned icon/text origins, preserving expanded fine-pointer positions and 44px touch controls. It also contains the 44px Shared control inside its strip, which previously projected 2px below it. All 44 header/sidebar Chromium and WebKit cases passed with unchanged existing goldens and 52 newly reviewed crops covering collapsed/empty groups, both themes, pointer modes and the 799/800px boundary. These are app consistency contracts, not measured Google pixel tokens. The muted indicator also remains visible after a DM moves into a custom section, including reload and unmute.

The current served public stylesheet and local computed styles both specify equal section-heading typography. They do not explain which document, zoom or cached assets produced the user's larger typography discrepancy. Google's Shared tab is also a separate files/links/media view; our Shared button still opens conversation details. That functional difference and exact hover styling remain open, rather than being hidden by a passing screenshot baseline. [Google's Shared reference](https://support.google.com/chat/answer/7651457?co=GENIE.Platform%3DDesktop&hl=en).

1. **Acceptance before additional promises:** run two independent accounts on two devices through text, group membership, image/audio round trips, reload, account switch and interrupted writes. Measure 10/30-person workloads with raw timing samples and state-consistency assertions. Local fixtures remain useful but cannot substitute for this.
2. **Find older conversations:** add authorized server-side pagination and search, with bounded queries, stable cursors, member isolation and UI loading/error states. This removes a daily-use limitation while retaining response budgets.
3. **Reach people when the app is closed:** design opt-in notifications with explicit delivery limits, subscription ownership, sign-out revocation, mute/DND semantics and permission-denial flows. Treat this as a separate feature requiring operational setup.
4. **Make invitations unmistakable:** clarify pending versus joined members and how to share the link. Consider actual transactional email only with a selected provider, abuse controls and delivery/retry evidence; do not simulate it through a misleading label.
5. **Prioritize writing essentials:** evaluate accessible formatting and quoted replies before scheduled send. Scheduled send needs a durable server scheduler, cancellation semantics and duplicate prevention, not a browser timer.
6. **Defer enterprise expansion:** AI assistants, hundreds of thousands of users, integration marketplaces and compliance consoles are outside the agreed scale. Do not create nonfunctional controls to resemble Google's broader service.

## Coverage is one measurement

The requested 95%+ target should be enforced against measured runtime execution with an explicit complete source inventory. Keep line, statement, function and branch results distinct. Type-only declarations cannot execute; they must be identified in the inventory rather than hidden or marked as tested. Preserve the old Node-only metric separately when adding browser execution, since different instrumentation can change the denominator.

Coverage does not measure assertion quality. Every new scenario should check an observable result, a denied operation, preserved state, resource cleanup or a failure/retry contract. The most valuable adversarial checks challenge the coverage pipeline itself: stale source maps, missing source files, uncollected browser pages, empty runs and accidentally counted dependency code must fail rather than produce a reassuring percentage.
